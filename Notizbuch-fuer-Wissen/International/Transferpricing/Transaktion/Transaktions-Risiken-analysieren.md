# Transaktions-Risiken analysieren

Contractual Recourse: A Key Concept in Transfer Pricing Analysis.

  


In the six OECD steps for delineating a controlled transaction with respect to risk, Step 1 is to “identify economically significant risks with specificity.”

  


Step 2 is to “determine how specific economically significant risks are contractually assumed by the associated enterprises under the terms of the transaction.”

  


This means looking at the actual terms of the controlled transaction from a contractual perspective.

‘Recourse’ is a useful concept here, and it refers to the legal right of one party to make a claim against another, and the amount which may be recovered.

  


Recourse is a function of the respective ‘Promises’ made by the parties, subject to any ‘Limitations’ on any claims which could be made in connection with those promises.

  


## Promises can include:

  


  * Duties (a promise to do something)
  * Reps and warranties (promises that certain factual statements are or will be true at a particular point in time)
  * Indemnities (promises to pay specified amounts in specified circumstances – such as an obligation to ‘make whole’ one party in the event of a claim by a third party)

  


## Limitations on claims can include:

  * Caps on claims (which may be expressed as monetary amounts)
  * Exclusions on claims (which can relate to excluded heads of claims, such as liability for loss of profits, or circumstances for which the promisor will not be responsible)

  


Assessing these factors involves looking at the totality of the contractual arrangements between the parties.

  


So unfortunately you need to read the whole of the relevant agreement, and also consider what implied terms may apply.

  


The resulting analysis is then subject to the ‘sanity check’ of Steps 3 to 5.

  


Which is really no more than common sense: it’s not meaningful for an entity to purport to assume a risk, if it does not have the financial capacity to do so, it has no ability to control or mitigate the risk, or if the contract does not match what the parties actually do.

  


It’s only after the transaction has been understood in this way, that it can be priced. That’s Step 6.

  


In reality, the contractual design of a controlled transaction goes hand-in-hand with the economic analysis.

  


Because the form and substance of a transaction is not dictated by circumstance.

  


It's a choice.
