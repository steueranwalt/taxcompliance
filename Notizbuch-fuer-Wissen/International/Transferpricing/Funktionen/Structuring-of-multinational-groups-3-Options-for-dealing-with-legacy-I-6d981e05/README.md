Structuring of multinational groups:

3 Options for dealing with legacy IP / acquired IP.

  


‘IP planning’ may be regarded a dirty word in some circles.

  


But the reality is that groups need to make a decision about how to manage their intellectual property (IP), and which entity or entities should hold the associated legal rights.

  


Doing nothing is a decision in itself.

  


The slide below attempts to show a situation where a group acquires IP. For the sake of simplicity, let’s say that the Parent entity (purple) acquires the shares of a company in another country, the IP Owner (blue).

Let’s say that the IP Owner holds rights in technology, which the Parent plans to exploit – and perhaps incorporate into its existing portfolio of solutions.

  


Let’s also say that the functions carried on by the IP Owner entity don’t change much before and after the acquisition. In particular, there’s an R&D team which continues to work in much the same way as before.

  


The group has to decide what to do with the acquired IP. Whatever it does, there will be intercompany transactions.

  


In reality, there’s an infinite number of options and permutations. The slide below attempts to show the three main options. Essentially (1) leave all the IP where it is, (2) move it all to the Parent, or (3) split the IP.

  


Each option needs to be assessed through multiple filters, including:

* Regulatory compliance, where applicable

* Government grants and government support, where applicable

* HR considerations, including whether the roles and/or physical location of individuals or teams will change

* Market and sales-related considerations, including which entity or entities will contract with third party customers

* Expectations of investors / future purchasers of the group, including the preferred location of IP rights

* Corporate governance, including complying with the duties of legal entity directors

* Administrative efficiency, including the cost of maintaining complex entity structures and fragmented IP ownership

* Accounting considerations, including what the accounts of the relevant entities will look like going forward

* Transfer pricing compliance, and the availability of data on comparable transactions

* Other tax considerations
